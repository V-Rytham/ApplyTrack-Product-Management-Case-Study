# ApplyTrack : A Product Management Case Study

---

## Why I worked on this case study

I recently completed four introductory Product Management courses on Coursera over approximately two months.

The coursework helped me understand how product decisions connect customer needs, business goals, and engineering execution. I learned about the end-to-end product lifecycle, stakeholder collaboration, market and competitive research, feature prioritization, roadmapping, and planning delivery and launch activities.

1. [Product Management: An Introduction](https://www.coursera.org/learn/product-management-an-introduction)

I wanted to take what I learned and apply it to a practical use case rather than leaving it at course notes and quizzes.

## How I arrived at ApplyTrack

I asked GPT how I could apply the product-management concepts to a practical use case. It suggested creating a product case study. I then explored a few possible ideas and selected ApplyTrack, a job-application tracking concept.

The choice came directly from a problem I experienced myself while applying to multiple companies across job boards, company career pages, and referral channels. After submitting dozens of applications, it quickly becomes difficult to track where you applied, which resume version was submitted, what the latest status is, and when to follow up.

I noticed that I was losing track of application timelines. Discussing this with peers confirmed that internships & job seekers often face the same friction, relying on fragmented tools like spreadsheets, emails, and sticky notes.

### Key Problem Areas

- **Information fragmentation:** Applications are scattered across job boards, emails, and personal notes.
- **Lost momentum & missed deadlines:** Follow-ups, assessments, and interview dates are easily missed.
- **Lack of visibility:** Without a unified dashboard, it takes unnecessary effort to assess active pipelines, response rates, and next actions.

## Case Study Structure & Approach

I applied foundational product management frameworks to structure the product development process from discovery through planning:

- **Problem Definition & Target Users:** Framing the user problem and defining target audience segments.
- **Customer Persona & Research:** Synthesizing peer input and community discussions into actionable user personas and pain points.
- **Competitive Analysis:** Reviewing existing market tools (e.g., Teal, Huntr, Simplify) to identify strengths and differentiation opportunities.
- **Product Vision & MVP Definition:** Establishing core value propositions and scoping a focused Minimum Viable Product.
- **Feature Prioritization:** Using structured prioritization methods (MoSCoW / Value vs. Effort) to determine MVP vs. future releases.
- **Product Roadmap:** Mapping a phased 3-month rollout from MVP launch to post-launch iterations.
- **Stakeholder Management & RACI:** Aligning engineering, design, marketing, and leadership responsibilities.
- **Success Metrics & KPIs:** Defining North Star and secondary metrics to measure engagement, retention, and funnel health.
- **Risks & Ethical Considerations:** Identifying data privacy risks, user trust considerations, and mitigation plans.
