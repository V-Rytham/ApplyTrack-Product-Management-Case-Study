## How I arrived at ApplyTrack

I asked GPT how I could apply the product-management concepts to a practical use case. It suggested creating a product case study. I then explored a few possible ideas and selected **ApplyTrack**, a job-application tracking concept.

The choice came from a problem I am experiencing myself. I am applying to multiple companies through different job portals, company career pages and other channels. After submitting applications, it can become difficult to remember where I applied, which role I selected, what the latest status is and whether I need to follow up.

I noticed that I was losing track of some applications. I also realized that this may not be an isolated experience: students and other job seekers often manage several applications at the same time, using a mix of portals, emails, spreadsheets and personal notes.

I recognize that the size of this problem may differ from person to person. Some people may find a spreadsheet sufficient, while others may find the scattered process genuinely difficult. For me, it is a meaningful problem because I am currently going through it.

### Why I consider it worth exploring

- **Information is spread out:** Applications can sit across several job portals, company websites, emails and referral conversations.
- **Important next steps are easy to lose track of:** Follow-ups, online assessments and interview dates may be recorded in different places.
- **It is hard to see the overall picture:** Without one record, it takes effort to understand how many applications are active, awaiting a response or completed.

These are my initial observations and hypotheses. They do not establish how common the problem is across all job seekers. That would require more user research.

## How I approached the case study

I used the product-management concepts from the courses to structure the idea. I also used GPT as a brainstorming and drafting aid. The product decisions and assumptions should be treated as a proposed case-study exercise, not as proof that the product has been built or validated.

The work was organized around:

- Defining the problem and identifying the intended users.
- Creating an illustrative customer persona.
- Reviewing existing job-search and application-tracking products.
- Considering user feedback and questions that could be explored through peer conversations.
- Defining a product vision and a limited first version (MVP).
- Prioritizing features and outlining a three-month roadmap.
- Mapping team responsibilities with a RACI matrix.
- Identifying success measures, risks and privacy considerations.

I also discussed the job-application tracking problem informally with a few peers and used the general themes I was exploring to shape an illustrative persona. The persona is not a real named participant, and it should not be read as a statistically representative research result. I have not included fabricated quotations, survey results or interview statistics.

## What this case study is — and is not

This is an independent learning exercise based on introductory product-management coursework and a problem I have personally encountered.

It is **not** a launched product, a completed commercial market study, or evidence of measured improvements in job-search outcomes. The roadmap, KPIs and feature priorities are proposals. They would need to be tested and revised using actual user feedback.

The goal was to practice thinking through a product problem in a structured way: understand the user, consider existing alternatives, decide what to build first, coordinate the work and think about how success and risk could be evaluated.
